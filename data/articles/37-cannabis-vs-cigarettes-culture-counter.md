# Cannabis vs. Cigarettes: National Numbers, Local Stigma at the Counter
**Dateline:** San Jose — September 2026
**Word count:** 718

*Federal survey tallies and a Gallup poll show a cultural pass for cannabis as cigarettes sit near historic lows. Prevalence is not a medical free pass.*

Two late-summer Bay Area television hits landed on the same cultural shift from different angles. On Aug. 5, 2026, ABC7 News Bay Area pointed to a federal study finding that marijuana use had surpassed cigarette smoking in the United States. On Sept. 3, KTVU reported that marijuana smoking among U.S. adults had reached a record high while cigarette smoking declined. Verified against open-web sources, those headlines hold—with measurement details shoppers should keep straight.

The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration’s 2025 National Survey on Drug Use and Health found that 43.8 million people ages 12 or older used marijuana in the past month, compared with 36.1 million current cigarette smokers in the same age span. ABC7’s write-up rounded that contrast to nearly 44 million marijuana users versus about 36 million cigarette smokers and noted that alcohol remained far more common, with nearly 130 million Americans reporting alcohol use. That comparison is past-month marijuana use of any kind—not only smoking—set against past-month cigarette smoking.

KTVU’s Sept. 3 package tracked Gallup’s annual Consumption Habits poll. Gallup reported that 17 percent of U.S. adults say they smoke marijuana, tying the poll’s record high first reached in 2023 and more than double the 7 percent reading when Gallup began asking in 2013. Cigarette smoking matched its record low at 11 percent. Separately, 15 percent of adults reported consuming marijuana edibles. The July 1–19, 2026, telephone survey of 1,200 adults underpins those percentages.

Together, the snapshots tell a consistent story even though the metrics differ: a large federal past-month survey ages 12 and up, and an adult poll of smoking and edible habits. Both show cannabis use ascending while cigarette smoking sits near historic lows.

In a city with a tightly limited set of registered cannabis storefronts—local reporting has placed the SJPD-registered business count near twenty—national normalization shows up as ordinary retail behavior. More adults treating cannabis like a known consumer good means more first-time questions at the counter and less whispered shopping. Workplaces, family gatherings, and federal rules still diverge from California’s adult-use framework, but the baseline has changed.

For South Bay dispensary regulars, that shift can lower social temperature without erasing health literacy. Cigarette smoking’s long decline followed decades of clear evidence about combustion and disease. Cannabis research is still catching up on products, potencies, and routes that barely existed in earlier eras. A cultural pass is not a medical free pass. Adults can acknowledge prevalence and still ask about labeling, dose, driving impairment, and smoke versus edible onset times. Say past-month marijuana use surpassed past-month cigarette smoking in the 2025 NSDUH estimates, not “everyone switched.” Keep alcohol in view: drinking still leads both categories by a wide margin. In San Jose’s limited storefront market, preparation beats impulse: verify the shop on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, bring ID, read the package yourself after any recommendation, and keep consumption on private property with permission. Adult-use sales require customers 21 and older. Tax totals at checkout still apply. None of those retail realities replace clinician advice when health questions are personal.
