# Heat Waves and Storage: Keeping Flower Fresh in a South Bay Summer
**Dateline:** San Jose — September 2026
**Word count:** 708

*Hot cars and sunny windowsills punish terpenes and cannabinoids. Cool, dark, airtight storage is the unglamorous half of a good eighth.*

South Bay summers bake dashboards, balconies, and garage shelves. Cannabis flower is an agricultural product with chemistry that does not love heat, light, oxygen, and wild humidity swings. Shoppers who spend carefully at licensed counters sometimes undo the jar between the parking lot and the couch.

Peer-reviewed storage research on cannabis secondary metabolites finds that cooler, darker, more stable conditions better preserve cannabinoid and terpene profiles over time, while heat and light accelerate degradation pathways—including shifts that can increase cannabinol as THC oxidizes. Industrial and forensic storage studies similarly warn that elevated temperature and oxygen exposure change composition. Consumer-facing guidance commonly lands near airtight, opaque containers, roughly mid-50s to low-60s percent relative humidity for short-term flower storage, and avoidance of hot cars—details that vary by source but share a theme: stop cooking your eighth.

San Jose practicalities are local. Do not leave a paper bag on a black car seat while you run errands downtown. Move product from the retail exit bag into a sealed jar at home. Keep jars out of west-facing windows. If you refrigerate for longer storage, understand condensation risk when cold jars are opened warm; many home growers and patients prefer stable room-cool cabinets over repeated fridge cycling. Freezing can preserve some chemistry but makes trichomes brittle—another reason casual shoppers may prefer simply buying amounts they will use sooner.

Concentrates, vapes, and edibles have their own heat problems. Cartridges can leak; chocolate can bloom or melt; beverages can sit forgotten in hot trunks. Child-resistant packaging still needs a cool location away from kids who explore cabinets during heat-wave indoor days. Wildfire weeks compound the issue when windows stay shut and indoor temperatures climb—pair lung-conscious product choices with storage discipline.

At the counter, ask staff which batches arrived recently if freshness matters to your nose. Smell, when stores allow, beats a THC percentage alone. Aroma fades when terpenes volatilize; that is chemistry, not a moral failing of a brand. Buy less more often if your apartment runs hot. Licensed retail receipts do not cool a glove box. The South Bay sun is free; replacing a dried-out eighth is not. In San Jose’s limited storefront market, preparation beats impulse: verify the shop on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, bring ID, read the package yourself after any recommendation, and keep consumption on private property with permission. Adult-use sales require customers 21 and older. Tax totals at checkout still apply. None of those retail realities replace clinician advice when health questions are personal.
