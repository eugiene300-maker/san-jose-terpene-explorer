*T-breaks are ordinary adult maintenance in a mature market—not a moral purge. Planning beats white-knuckle improvisation.*

In tech-heavy San Jose, people reboot phones, clear caches, and joke about inbox zero. Cannabis tolerance breaks—“t-breaks”—fit the same maintenance culture. Regulars notice they need more flower for the same effect, edibles feel muted, or morning fog lingers. A reset is how adults answer without pretending abstinence is the only virtuous story.

There is no single medically mandated break length for adult-use shoppers. Conversations at licensed counters and among peers often range from a few days to a few weeks, depending on prior dose, format, and goals. Higher-potency concentrate users sometimes describe longer resets than weekend flower consumers. The honest local tone skips shame: you are recalibrating, not confessing.

Practical planning matters more than slogans. Pick a start date that does not collide with travel stress or major deadlines if you can help it. Remove easy-access product from the nightstand—or ask a trusted adult roommate to hold it if that helps, with clear consent. Expect sleep and appetite changes for some people; those are reasons to be kind to yourself, not to redose in panic at 2 a.m. Exercise, hydration, and ordinary hobbies fill time better than doomscrolling dispensary menus.

When you return, do not resume at your old concentrate ceiling. Restart with lower-THC flower, a smaller edible, or fewer cart pulls. The point of a break evaporates if day one is a revenge dab. Notes help: what dose felt right after the reset becomes next month’s baseline.

Budtenders can suggest lower-potency SKUs for a soft landing. They should not police your break or invent medical withdrawal protocols. If cannabis use feels compulsive, entangled with mental-health crises, or impossible to pause, that is clinician territory—not a newspaper shopping tip. For ordinary tolerance creep in a legal adult market, the San Jose vernacular is practical: pause, reset, restart lower, keep receipts and ID habits intact when you shop again. In San Jose’s limited storefront market, preparation beats impulse: verify the shop on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, bring ID, read the package yourself after any recommendation, and keep consumption on private property with permission. Adult-use sales require customers 21 and older. Tax totals at checkout still apply. None of those retail realities replace clinician advice when health questions are personal.
