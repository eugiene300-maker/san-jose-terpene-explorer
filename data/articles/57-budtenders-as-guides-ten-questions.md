# Budtenders as Guides: Ten Questions Worth Asking

*In a scarce market with overlapping menus, good questions turn cashiers into guides—without turning them into doctors.*

In a San Jose market where many licensed shops carry overlapping brands, the person behind the counter becomes part of the product. Budtenders are not doctors and should not be asked for diagnoses. Done well, they are local guides who translate a crowded menu into a few honest options based on format, aroma, onset, and budget.

Ten questions worth asking: (1) What formats do you suggest for a beginner versus a heavy-tolerant regular? (2) What is the THC per serving on this edible, and is it traditional or fast-acting? (3) Which current flower batches lean pine, citrus, or dessert on the nose? (4) Is this pre-roll flower or shake, classic or infused? (5) For carts, is this distillate, live resin, or another extract type? (6) Do you have CBD-forward or 1:1 options in stock today? (7) How fresh is this batch, and where do I find the test date? (8) What is the out-the-door price with tax? (9) What is your policy on returns or recalls if a product fails? (10) Are there quiet hours when you recommend first-timers visit?

Good guidance includes saying no. Staff should steer inexperienced shoppers away from high-milligram edibles or heavy infused blunts when the customer’s story suggests caution. Shoppers should bring constraints—“I got too high last month,” “I need something for a private quiet night,” “I cannot have a strong smell”—instead of only asking for whatever is popular on social media.

Local coverage of new retail openings has emphasized welcoming floor staff and career paths that started at the counter—useful culture without turning any single chain into a tour feature. Early hours sometimes mean shorter lines. Bag policies still apply. ID checks for adults 21 and older are non-negotiable. Recommendations are not prescriptions. Tip if policy and budget allow. Then leave with a plan that fits your tolerance—not the loudest demo on a patio. In San Jose’s limited storefront market, preparation beats impulse: verify the shop on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, bring ID, read the package yourself after any recommendation, and keep consumption on private property with permission. Adult-use sales require customers 21 and older. Tax totals at checkout still apply. None of those retail realities replace clinician advice when health questions are personal.
