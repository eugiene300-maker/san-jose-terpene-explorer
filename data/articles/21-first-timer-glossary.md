# First-Timer Glossary: Flower, Preroll, Cart, Rosin, RSO

A first visit to a licensed San Jose cannabis retailer can feel like landing in a new dialect. Menus mix chemistry, slang, and brand names. This glossary covers five formats shoppers actually meet at the counter—without turning the trip into a chemistry seminar.

Flower is dried cannabis buds sold by weight, typically in eighths (about 3.5 grams) or other listed amounts. Labels show THC and CBD percentages by weight for the batch. Flower is smoked or vaporized in a dry-herb device. It is the baseline format many people compare everything else against. Smell, moisture, and grind affect the experience as much as the percentage on the jar.

A preroll is flower (sometimes mixed with concentrate) rolled into a ready-to-smoke cigarette-style product. Convenience is the pitch. Quality varies: some prerolls use the same flower you would buy loose; others use trim. Infused prerolls include concentrate and hit harder—read the milligrams if listed and start slow.

A cart (cartridge) is usually a distillate or other extract in a sealed unit that attaches to a battery. “Live resin” or “rosin” carts claim different extraction styles; prices track those claims. Counterfeit carts outside licensed shops have been a national safety story—buy only sealed, labeled hardware from SJPD-registered retailers. Batteries matter: use the hardware the product was designed for, and do not charge unattended on a couch cushion.

Rosin is a concentrate made by pressing cannabis with heat and pressure, typically without hydrocarbon solvents. “Live rosin” starts from fresh-frozen material. Rosin is often dabbed or used in devices designed for concentrates; potency is high compared with flower. A rice-grain-sized amount can be plenty for a new concentrate consumer.

RSO (Rick Simpson Oil style preparations and similarly labeled full-extract products) appears in some medical-leaning conversations as a highly concentrated oil, often in syringes. Potency and intended use vary. This is not a DIY instruction article—ask a budtender how a specific labeled product is meant to be measured, and talk with a clinician if you are using cannabis alongside medications. Do not invent homemade solvent extracts; volatile solvents are dangerous and outside consumer retail norms.

Other words you will hear: terpenes (aromatic compounds that shape smell and may influence effect), indica/sativa (folk labels that are imperfect predictors in modern hybrid markets), and mg THC on edibles (milligrams of delta-9 THC per package and per serving—California adult-use packages are capped by regulation).

First-timer habits that beat jargon: tell the budtender your experience level; ask for lower-THC flower or a 2.5–5 mg edible serving if you are new; buy less than you think you need; keep the receipt; and check the retailer on SJPD’s registered list. Formats are tools. Start with the tool you can measure.
