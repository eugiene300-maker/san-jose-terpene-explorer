# How to Read a California Cannabis Label (THC, Terpenes, Batch)

*The sticker is the product’s résumé—THC, CBD, batch ID, and warnings beat the strain nickname on the shelf talker.*

California’s legal cannabis label is denser than a soda bottle and more useful than a strain nickname. Department of Cannabis Control labeling rules require licensed goods to carry identity, potency, and tracking information so consumers can see what they bought and regulators can follow the batch.

Start with the product identity: name, type (flower, edible, vape, topical), net weight or volume, and the manufacturer or distributor information the package lists. Adult-use packages carry an adult-use designation; medicinal packages carry medicinal designation. UID or batch identifiers link the item to California’s track-and-trace system—keep that string if you ever need to report a problem.

Potency is usually listed as THC and CBD percentages for flower and concentrates, or as milligrams of THC (and CBD, if claimed) per serving and per package for edibles. California edible rules limit how much THC can appear in a single package and require clear serving sizes. If a gummy bag says 100 mg total with 10 mg servings, that is ten servings—not one casual snack. First-timers should treat milligrams seriously; delayed onset causes many overconsumption stories.

Terpene listings, when present, are optional marketing-adjacent chemistry: myrcene, limonene, caryophyllene, and others describe aroma more reliably than they guarantee a mood. Use them as a nose guide, not a medical claim. Ignore informal medical promises on a sticker; licensed packaging must follow warning and claim rules.

Mandatory warnings matter. California packages carry health warnings about impairment, delayed edible effects, keeping products away from children, and other required statements. The universal symbol for cannabis goods is a quick visual cue that the item is regulated cannabis—not a vitamin gummy.

What the label cannot do: tell you how you personally will feel after a stressful workday, guarantee sleep, or erase apartment no-smoking clauses. What it can do: help you compare strength, avoid accidental double dosing, and identify a batch during a recall.

San Jose shopping tip: photograph the label of products you like. In a scarce storefront market, the same brand may appear at multiple registered retailers; matching batch dates and packaging helps you compare freshness. If a social-media “menu” shows product without California labeling cues, walk away.

Reading the label is the adult version of smelling a jar through glass. The nickname gets you to the shelf. The sticker decides whether the purchase matches your plan.
