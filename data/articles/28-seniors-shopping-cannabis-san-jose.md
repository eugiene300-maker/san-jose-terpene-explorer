*Start low, go slow, read milligrams, and bring a clinician into the conversation when medications are involved.*

San Jose’s senior population includes longtime medical patients, curious newcomers, and adults managing sleep, appetite, or discomfort who want clear information without youth-culture marketing. Licensed retail can serve that audience—if pacing and labeling lead the visit.

Start with registration and ID. Adult-use purchases require age 21 and older; medicinal purchases with a physician’s recommendation can begin at 18 under state rules, which matters more for younger caregivers than for seniors themselves. The retailer should appear on SJPD’s registered list. Bring a clear government ID and, if shopping medicinal, current recommendation paperwork.

Edibles deserve special respect. California labels list THC in milligrams per serving and per package. Delayed onset—often one to two hours or more—causes many first-timer overconsumption stories. A 2.5 mg or 5 mg serving is a wiser first experiment than a 50 mg chocolate bar. “Start low, go slow” is cliché because it is true. Flower and vapor hit faster; that can be easier to titrate for some people and harsher on lungs for others. Discuss lung and heart concerns with a clinician, not only a budtender.

Medications matter. Cannabis can interact with prescriptions. Budtenders can explain product formats; they are not your pharmacist or physician. Bring a medication list to a clinician before mixing THC with sedatives, blood thinners, or other drugs. This paper will not invent interaction charts.

Practical senior-friendly shopping habits:
1. Visit at mid-morning on weekdays when stores are quieter.
2. Ask staff to explain label milligrams out loud.
3. Prefer child-resistant storage even in empty-nest homes—grandkids visit.
4. Budget for San Jose’s tax stack; legal prices surprise first-timers.
5. Arrange a ride if you plan to sample; do not drive impaired.

Mobility and sensory needs are legitimate. Ask about seating, quieter corners, or larger-print menus if available. Accessibility varies by location in San Jose’s industrial-corridor retail map.

Seniors also face stigma in both directions—dismissed as too old for cannabis or pressured by wellness hype. Neutral journalism’s job is neither. Legal retail offers tested product and labels. Your job is pacing, medical consultation when needed, and private consumption that respects leases and roommates.

A calm first purchase is a small amount of a clearly labeled product from a registered shop, not a bulk deal inspired by a coupon board. Curiosity is allowed. Rushing is optional.
